# 口语素材 — 地点组：大阪

## Part 2: Favorite City You Visited（核心回答）

I'd like to talk about Osaka, hands down my favorite city. It's in the Kansai region of Japan — famous for street food, shopping districts, and a really vibrant vibe.

I first got curious about Osaka through those street food walking tours on Bilibili. After watching a bunch, I really wanted to see what daily life in a Japanese city actually feels like.

I went there in June 2025, right after graduation — kind of a celebration trip. It was a one-week Japan trip with a close friend. We landed in Osaka and spent the first few days there, staying near Shinsaibashi. Then we went to Kyoto for a bit, and took a night bus from Osaka all the way to the Tokyo area. But honestly, Osaka was the highlight of the whole trip.

The most memorable spot was the Umeda Sky Building. I went up to the floating observatory — you're standing between two towers — and the night view was breathtaking. The whole city spread out beneath us, this endless grid of lights stretching to the horizon. That was the moment I realized Osaka was way bigger than I'd pictured.

So yeah, looking back, Osaka stuck with me not just because it was impressive — but because of what it meant at that time. It felt like a reward for four years of university. Like closing one chapter and starting another.

## 2. Tall Building You Like — 梅田蓝天大厦

I'd like to talk about the Umeda Sky Building in Osaka, which is probably the most impressive building I've seen in real life.

It's a twin-tower structure in the Umeda district — two separate towers connected at the very top by this floating observatory. It looks like a giant glass bridge suspended in the air. I went there during my Osaka trip in June 2025, right after graduation.

The coolest part is the elevator ride up to the observatory. You go through this glass tube between the towers, and as you rise, the city just unfolds beneath you. When I stepped out, the night view was breathtaking — the whole city spread out like a carpet of lights stretching to the horizon.

I think the main reason I liked it so much is that it gave me a completely different perspective on the city. From street level, Osaka is neon signs and narrow alleys and food stalls — super lively but kind of chaotic. From up there, it looked perfectly ordered, like someone had laid out every road and building on purpose. That contrast really stuck with me.

So yeah, it wasn't just a tall building — it was the moment I realized Osaka was way bigger and more modern than I'd ever imagined.

## 3. Tall Building You Dislike — 同一栋楼，从街上看

I'd like to talk about the Umeda Sky Building in Osaka — but from a different perspective. When I saw it from the street, I honestly thought it was kind of ugly.

We were walking from Osaka Station towards the building, and from below, the lower half looks like a generic office block from the 90s — grey concrete, no character, just boxy and dated. My friend actually said, "This is the famous one?" and I was thinking the same thing.

What made it worse was the long walk to get there. It's about 15 minutes from the station through this underground tunnel, and when you finally come out, the first thing you see is... a parking lot. Not exactly a grand entrance.

But here's the thing — once we went inside and got up to the floating observatory, my opinion completely flipped. The view was stunning and the whole sky garden concept was really cool. So I guess I disliked the building from the outside, but loved the experience inside.

I mention this because it taught me something — you really can't judge a building, or honestly anything, from first impressions alone.

## 4. Boring Place — Willer 夜巴候车厅

I'd like to talk about the Willer night bus waiting area in Osaka, which was honestly one of the dullest places I've ever been stuck in.

This was during my Japan trip in June 2025. We were taking an overnight bus from Osaka to the Tokyo area — a Willer bus, which is a pretty popular budget option for traveling between cities in Japan. We arrived at the waiting area around 9 p.m., and the bus wasn't leaving until close to midnight. So we had over two hours to kill.

The problem was, there was almost nothing there. It was this small indoor space with maybe twenty hard plastic chairs bolted to the floor, a couple of vending machines, and one dim restroom. That was it. No café, no shops, no comfortable seating — just a room with chairs and a departure screen. Compared to the rest of Osaka — where every street corner had something going on — this place felt like a void.

What made it worse was that there weren't even enough seats for everyone. Some people were sitting on the floor next to their suitcases. The Wi-Fi was spotty, so I couldn't even watch videos to pass the time. We just sat there, checking the departure screen every five minutes, waiting for our bus number to show up.

So yeah, the trip itself was amazing, but those two hours in the Willer waiting area were painfully boring. It felt like time had just... stopped.

## 5. Car Trip You Would Like to Go — 想自驾走一趟日本

I'd like to talk about a car trip I'd really love to do someday — driving along the coast of Japan.

I got this idea during my Japan trip in June 2025. We traveled between cities by train and night bus — Osaka to Kyoto, then Osaka all the way to Tokyo. And while the trains were incredibly efficient, I kept looking out the window and seeing these small coastal towns, fishing villages, mountains in the distance — places you'd never get to if you only travel between major cities by public transport.

Who I'd go with — probably one close friend, someone who can share the driving. I wouldn't want a big group because then you'd have to coordinate everyone's schedules and preferences. Just two people, a car, and a rough plan.

Where and when — I'd start from Osaka, drive along the coast towards Hiroshima, maybe continue down to Kyushu. I'd want to do it in spring or autumn, when the weather is mild and the scenery is at its best. Maybe a week or so, no tight schedule.

Why by car — because it gives you total freedom. On my trip, I loved the cities, but I felt like I only saw 30% of what Japan actually looks like. The train is point A to point B. A car lets you stop whenever something catches your eye — a small shrine, a roadside noodle shop, a random beach. That's the kind of travel I want to try next.

I loved the food too — okonomiyaki, kushikatsu, fresh sushi from random basement food halls. Every meal felt like an adventure.

The most memorable spot was the Umeda Sky Building. I went up to the floating observatory and the night view was breathtaking — the whole city spread out beneath us, this endless grid of lights stretching to the horizon.

So yeah, I honestly enjoyed every minute of being there.

## 9. Place to Visit in Free Time — 环球影城

I'd like to talk about Universal Studios Japan in Osaka, which is a place I'd really love to visit if I had more free time.

I knew about it before my trip in June 2025, but we didn't have time to go — we only had a few days in Osaka and there was just too much else to see. We actually walked past the entrance on our way somewhere else, and I saw the giant spinning globe and the crowd pouring in, and I was like, man, I wish we had one more day.

Part of the reason I want to go is Super Nintendo World — it's basically a life-sized Mario game. You walk through a green pipe and suddenly you're in Mushroom Kingdom. I've watched so many videos of people riding the Mario Kart ride and it looks absolutely wild. They have these AR goggles and you're throwing shells at other racers while driving through the actual Mario world.

The other thing is, going to a theme park in Japan is a completely different experience. I've heard the staff there bow and wave and smile like they're genuinely happy you're there, and the food is actually good — not just overpriced hot dogs, but themed meals that people actually want to eat.

So yeah, I didn't make it there on my trip, but it's definitely on my list for next time. If I had a free weekend and a plane ticket, I'd go.

## 10. Interesting Video — B 站旅行 vlog

I'd like to talk about a travel vlog I watched on Bilibili before my trip to Osaka, which ended up shaping my whole trip.

I watched it sometime in May 2025, about a month before I went. It was a 20-minute walking tour video of Shinsaibashi and Dotonbori at night — no narration, no music, just a guy walking through the streets with a camera. I had never been to Osaka before, and the video made it feel so vivid that I was absolutely glued to the screen. In the end I didn't actually make it to Dotonbori on the trip — we ran out of time — but the video still shaped how I imagined the city before I got there.

The video showed the famous Glico running man sign, the giant crab above the restaurant, all the street food stalls with steam rising from the grills, people lining up for takoyaki, the neon signs reflected in the canal. It was so vivid that I felt like I was already there.

I watched it because I was planning my trip and trying to figure out which areas were worth staying in. But honestly, after watching it, I wasn't just informed — I was genuinely excited. It turned my trip from an idea into something real.

The interesting thing was, when I actually got to Shinsaibashi in June, everything looked exactly like it did in the video — except it was louder, busier, and smelled incredible. It felt like stepping into a screen. So yeah, that video didn't just help me plan — it made me count down the days until I could see it for real.
