## 1. Technology You Would Like to Own（核心回答）

I'd like to talk about the iPad I bought recently, which is honestly one of the best purchases I've made.

It's an iPad 7 — not the latest model, which helped keep the cost down. What sold me was seeing how students use it with the Apple Pencil — they'd annotate directly on practice tests, highlight passages, erase and rewrite. Compared to printing out stacks of paper every week, it looked so much more efficient.

Why would I like to own it — well, I already own it now, and I can confirm it's been incredibly useful. I use it for everything: reading practice passages, taking notes on vocabulary, doing writing tasks with split screen where the question is on one side and my essay on the other. It's also great for watching English videos without subtitles, which I do for listening practice.

It's not just a gadget, honestly. It's become my main study tool. I know some people buy iPads and then just use them for watching shows, but for me, it's genuinely transformed how I study. Best investment I made for this exam.

## 2. Program or App on Your Computer or Phone

I'd like to talk about Notability, which is a note-taking app I use on my iPad almost every day.

It's basically a digital notebook — you can write with a stylus, type, highlight, insert images, record audio, all in one place. I first found out about it when I was researching iPad apps for studying. Almost every student review video recommended it.

I use it every day, usually for about two to three hours. When I'm doing reading practice, I import the PDF into Notability and annotate directly on it — underline key sentences, circle unfamiliar words, write margin notes. When I'm doing writing practice, I create a new note, set up a split screen with the essay question on one side and a blank page on the other, and just write. It's way faster than paper because I can move paragraphs around and correct mistakes without making a mess.

How I feel about it? Honestly, it's one of those apps that just works. It's not flashy or complicated. It does what it's supposed to do and stays out of your way. I think that's the highest compliment you can give a tool — you forget it's even there because you're so focused on the actual work.

## 3. Item You Spent More Than Expected On

I'd like to talk about something I ended up spending way more on than I planned, which was my iPad setup for studying.

So I bought the iPad itself — the iPad 7, which was reasonably priced compared to the Pro models. That was the planned expense. What I didn't budget for was everything else. A stylus for taking notes — another few hundred. A decent case with a keyboard was around 400. Then a screen protector. Then I realized I needed cloud storage because my notes were piling up. And the note-taking app I wanted wasn't free — it was a subscription.

So what I thought would be a straightforward purchase ended up costing quite a bit more. Each extra felt justified at the time, but they added up fast.

Why did I spend more than expected? Partly because I didn't do my research on accessories — I just assumed they'd be cheap. But also because, once you've invested in the main device, it's really hard to say no to the accessories that make it actually usable. Like, you can't really take notes without the Pencil. You can't really type essays without a keyboard. Each purchase felt justified at the time, but they added up fast.

I don't regret it, honestly. The setup has been worth every yuan for my studies. But it definitely taught me to factor in the full cost before buying any tech.

## 5. Advertisement with a Famous Person 🆕

I'd like to talk about an advertisement I saw that featured a famous person, which was actually an iPad Pro commercial.

The ad featured a well-known Chinese director — I think it was part of Apple's "Behind the Scenes" campaign. The ad showed him sketching storyboards on an iPad Pro, reviewing footage, even editing rough cuts — all on this one device. It wasn't a typical ad where a celebrity just holds up a product and smiles. He was actually using it.

I saw it online, probably on Weibo or Bilibili, around the time I was researching which iPad to buy. And I have to admit, it was pretty effective. Here was someone at the top of a creative field, using the same device I was considering, and making it look completely natural — not like a gimmick, but like a genuine professional tool.

How did I feel about it? It definitely influenced me, even though I knew I was being marketed to. The ad didn't try to sell me on specs or features. It just showed someone doing real work, and let you draw your own conclusion. I think that's the most persuasive kind of advertising — the kind where you convince yourself.

That said, I ended up getting the iPad Air, not the Pro. So the ad worked, but not exactly as intended.
